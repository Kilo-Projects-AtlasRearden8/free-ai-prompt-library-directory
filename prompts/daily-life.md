# Daily Life Prompts

Starter prompts for everyday admin: meals, gifts, packing, and low-energy productivity. Written to be ADHD-friendly — small steps, plain language.

---

## Meal plan from what's already in your pantry

**Use case:** You have random ingredients and need dinner ideas without going to the store.

**Example prompt:**
```
I have these ingredients on hand: {{INGREDIENTS}}. I eat {{DIET}} and have about {{TIME}} minutes to cook. Suggest 3 simple dinners using mostly what I have, plus a short grocery list of anything missing (5 items max). Keep steps to 5 or fewer per recipe. Tone: encouraging, low pressure.
```

**Steps:**
1. List what's actually in your fridge and pantry in `{{INGREDIENTS}}`.
2. Note any diet needs and how much time you have.
3. Paste into any free AI chat.
4. Pick one recipe and cook it; cross off ingredients as you use them.

## Gift ideas for someone hard to shop for

**Use case:** You need present ideas for a person, occasion, and budget.

**Example prompt:**
```
Suggest 5 gift ideas for {{RECIPIENT}} for {{OCCASION}}. Budget: around ${{BUDGET}}. They like {{INTERESTS}} and they live in {{LOCATION}}. Mix of physical gifts, experiences, and one handmade or low-cost idea. For each idea: one sentence on why it fits and where to look for it. No accounts or memberships required.
```

**Steps:**
1. Fill in recipient, occasion, budget, interests, and location.
2. Paste into any free AI chat.
3. Pick one idea, order or make it, and you're done.

## Packing list for a trip

**Use case:** You're traveling and don't want to forget essentials — or overpack.

**Example prompt:**
```
Create a packing list for a {{DURATION}}-day trip to {{DESTINATION}} in {{SEASON}}. Activities: {{ACTIVITIES}}. Group items into: clothes, toiletries, electronics/documents, and extras. Include a "if you forget it" section (things you can buy there). Keep it short enough to screenshot.
```

**Steps:**
1. Fill in duration, destination, season, and activities.
2. Paste into any free AI chat and screenshot the result.
3. Check each item off as you pack it.

## Low-energy task starter (ADHD-friendly)

**Use case:** You're stuck and need the tiniest possible first step to start a task.

**Example prompt:**
```
I need to start {{TASK}} but I'm low on energy today. Break it into 5 steps so small they feel almost silly (each under 5 minutes). The first step must take under 2 minutes. Tone: gentle and encouraging, never shaming. End with one sentence of permission to stop after the first step.
```

**Steps:**
1. Name the task you're avoiding in `{{TASK}}`.
2. Paste into any free AI chat.
3. Do step one only. Stop if you need to — that still counts.

## Budget from a bank statement

**Use case:** You have a month of bank transactions and want a simple, honest budget you can actually follow — low-energy, ADHD-friendly.

**Example prompt:**
```
Here are my bank transactions for {{MONTH}}. Group them into categories (Groceries, Rent/Housing, Transport, Subscriptions, Eating Out, Transfers/Savings, Other) and tell me the total per category. Then write a plain-English summary: what I spent the most on, and one small suggestion for next month that feels doable. No shame, no jargon. Mask any account numbers, customer names, or payment details before pasting.
{{TRANSACTIONS}}
```

**Steps:**
1. Export one month of transactions as CSV or copy a few rows.
2. Mask anything personal before pasting.
3. Paste into any free AI chat.
4. Copy the category totals into a simple spreadsheet or note.
5. Change one small thing next month — that's the whole plan.

## Organize digital paperwork

**Use case:** You have a messy folder of scanned receipts, PDFs, and photos and want a simple naming and folder plan.

**Example prompt:**
```
I have digital files for {{PURPOSE}} (receipts, invoices, medical records, etc.). Suggest a simple folder structure and a naming convention that works on a phone and a computer. Include: how many folders, what to name files (date-first so they sort), what to delete or archive, and a 5-minute starter routine. Keep it to one screen of text.
```

**Steps:**
1. Pick one small pile of files to start with — not everything at once.
2. Create the folders the prompt suggests.
3. Rename files date-first (YYYY-MM-DD what-it-is).
4. Delete or archive anything you won't need this year.
